## **INTERIM RESULTS ANNOUNCEMENT FOR THE SIX MONTHS ENDED 30 SEPTEMBER 2026 AND CHANGES TO THE COMPOSITION OF BOARD COMMITTEES**

The board of directors (the **Board** ) of Link Asset Management Limited ( **Link** ), as manager of Link Real Estate Investment Trust ( **Link REIT** ), is pleased to report to unitholders of Link REIT (the **Unitholders** ) the unaudited interim results of Link REIT and its subsidiaries (the **Group** ) for the six months ended 30 September 2026 ( **1H 2026/2027** ).

The interim results and the condensed consolidated interim financial information of the Group for the six months ended 30 September 2026, after review by the Audit and Risk Management Committee of Link (the **Audit and Risk Management Committee** ), were approved by the Board on 12 November 2026.

### **OVERALL FINANCIAL RESULTS**

Total distributable amount for 1H 2026/2027 increased by 0.4% to HK$3,295 million from HK$3,283 million in the same period last financial year ( **1H 2025/2026** ). Distribution per unit remained broadly stable at HK127.25 cents in 1H 2026/2027, compared with HK126.88 cents in 1H 2025/2026.

Revenue in 1H 2026/2027 amounted to HK$6,909 million, representing a 1.6% decrease from HK$7,023 million in 1H 2025/2026, mainly due to negative rental reversions in Hong Kong, reflecting the lagging recovery of non-discretionary retail, alongside the continued evolution of online consumption patterns. Net property income was HK$5,135 million, down 0.8% from HK$5,178 million in 1H 2025/2026. The net property income margin improved in 1H 2026/2027, mainly driven by disciplined operating cost management and ongoing efficiency initiatives.

1

General and administrative expenses reduced by 20.8% to HK$365 million (1H 2025/2026: HK$461 million), reflecting the full-period benefit of the organisational streamlining measures undertaken in the last financial year. Net finance costs, excluding fair value changes that do not affect distributable income, increased by 5.8% year on year, mainly due to higher interest rates.

Total property portfolio valuation as at 30 September 2026 decreased by 1.5%, or HK$3,191 million, to HK$213,298 million (31 March 2026: HK$216,489 million), mainly reflecting the impact of asset disposals and fair value movements, partially offset by foreign currency appreciation against the Hong Kong dollar. During the period, Swing By @ Thomson Plaza in Singapore and a 50% interest in 100 Market Street in Sydney were disposed of for S$250 million and A$226 million, respectively.

Net assets attributable to Unitholders declined by 1.7% to HK$147,559 million (31 March 2026: HK$150,096 million), and net asset value per unit fell by 1.3% to HK$56.99 (31 March 2026: HK$57.75). Net gearing ratio as at 30 September 2026 was 23.3% (31 March 2026: 23.9%).

### **MANAGEMENT DISCUSSION AND ANALYSIS**

### **Operational Highlights – China**

Hong Kong Portfolio

As at 30 September 2026, Link REIT’s Hong Kong portfolio comprised 130 assets, characterised by non-discretionary retail spaces, fresh markets and around 57,000 car parking spaces near public housing estates and major transport links. These properties are pivotal to the local retail infrastructure and offer resilient income streams throughout economic cycles. Their strategic location ensures easy access to day-to-day goods, services and parking for both estate residents and visitors. This portfolio also included a 60% stake in The Quayside, an office asset located in Kowloon East, and two car park/car service centres and godown buildings in Hung Hom and Chai Wan.

During the period under review, Hong Kong’s retail market continued its gradual recovery, with retail sales growing across both discretionary and non-discretionary segments. The pace, — however, remained uneven discretionary categories, supported by improving consumer sentiment and a steadier inbound visitor base, outpaced non-discretionary categories. Evolving purchasing behaviour towards e-commerce and the continued expansion of online channels exerted further pressure on traditional retail formats, reinforcing the need for physical retail to compete on convenience, service and experience.

Within this operating context, our Hong Kong portfolio recorded year-on-year declines of 2.7% in revenue and 2.9% in net property income. Revenue remained constrained by negative rental reversions, though proactive cost-saving initiatives and disciplined expense management lowered property operating expenses by 2.4%, partly cushioning the impact at the net property income level.

Looking beyond the near term, we are actively curating our tenant mix towards service and experience-led offerings that are better placed to capture evolving consumer demand and less susceptible to online substitution. This will be reinforced by investments to modernise our core — — assets in the portfolio enhancing arrival experience, ambience and circulation which we believe can help drive longer dwell time, higher footfall, and as a result better tenant sales.

### **Retail**

- The Hong Kong retail portfolio accounts for over half of our total revenue. As at 30 September 2026, its occupancy stood at 97.0%, underpinned by a defensive tenant mix and stable demand from daily-needs retailers.

- Amid competitive market dynamics, retail rental reversion was negative 7.5% for 1H 2026/2027. Average monthly unit rent as at 30 September 2026 moderated to HK$59.4 per square foot ( **psf** ) from HK$60.1 psf as at 31 March 2026.

- Tenant sales were broadly stable, declining slightly by 0.2% year on year for 1H 2026/2027, while the rent-to-sales ratio was 12.4%. By segment, food and beverage recorded 2.5% year-on-year sales growth, while supermarket and foodstuff and general retail declined 2.2% and 1.4% year on year, respectively.

- During the period under review, our leasing team signed over 100 new brands across the portfolio. Leasing demand was led by learning and interest classes, specialty restaurants, fashion accessories, light refreshments and household utensils, reflecting evolving consumer preferences. Tenant retention remained strong at around 82%.

- In response to evolving market dynamics, we continued to curate experience-driven — —

We also increased the use of common areas for activations, including food fairs and pop-up events, to drive footfall and generate incremental revenue. Collectively, these initiatives strengthened our centres’ role as everyday community destinations, differentiated by experiences and services that e-commerce cannot readily replicate.

- Capital expenditure of approximately HK$800 million has been earmarked for projects currently under planning and statutory approval stages for the remainder of 2026/2027 to 2028/2029. Separately, asset enhancement projects underway as at 30 September 2026 represented total capital expenditure of HK$116 million and are expected to be completed between October 2026 and April 2027.

- Four asset enhancement projects are in the pipeline for the second half of 2026/2027 at Choi Wan, Po Tat, Tin Chak and Hing Tung, involving estimated capital expenditure of HK$26 million, HK$24 million, HK$24 million and HK$19 million, respectively.

### **Car Parks and Related Business**

- Car parks and related business revenue, which accounts for nearly one-fifth of our total revenue, was flat year on year when excluding free parking. Monthly car park income decreased by 1.6% year on year, while hourly income (including new income initiatives and excluding free parking) increased by 4.2% year on year.

7

- In 1H 2026/2027, revenue per car park space per month was HK$3,291. As at 30 September 2026, average car park valuation per space was approximately HK$692,000, decreasing by 1.8% (31 March 2026: HK$705,000).

- Parking demand remained soft amid declining car ownership and lower ticket volumes. To drive utilisation and strengthen recurring parking income, we implemented a series of targeted initiatives across the car park portfolio. These included the revamp of parking privilege programmes, converting selected free parking benefits into discounted tariff offerings, as well as the reconfiguration of suitable areas within existing car parks to create additional motorcycle parking spaces, capturing growing demand and generating incremental income.

- In addition, we leveraged data analytics and dynamic pricing strategies to optimise parking tariffs based on demand patterns, location characteristics and utilisation during peak periods. Targeted hourly parking promotions for taxi drivers were also introduced to attract demand and improve utilisation during off-peak hours.

Chinese Mainland Portfolio

As at 30 September 2026, Link REIT’s Chinese Mainland portfolio comprised six retail assets, an office and five logistics assets in tier-one cities and the surrounding river delta areas. These assets are strategically located to capitalise on the dense population and extensive economic activities of their local catchments. The assets are poised to benefit from these regions’ promising long-term growth prospects, solid consumer demands and dynamic commercial activities.

China’s macro economy continues its structural shift towards higher-quality, consumption-driven growth, supported by a renewed policy focus on domestic demand. However, the retail operating environment remains challenging. Consumer sentiment is still subdued, and demand has yet to fully recover. Competition from both physical and online retail remains intense, adding pressure to the operating environment. The pace of recovery also remains uneven across the tier-one cities in which we operate. Nonetheless, the experience-led offerings and well-curated food and beverage mix across our Chinese Mainland portfolio have helped to provide some support to footfall and tenant sales.

During 1H 2026/2027, total revenue and net property income of our Chinese Mainland portfolio decreased by 2.9% and 1.2%, respectively, in Renminbi terms. Similar to Hong Kong, the decrease in revenue reflected negative rental reversions, while our cost efficiency initiatives and organisational streamlining delivered meaningful savings, cushioning much of the impact at the net property income level. In Hong Kong dollar terms after currency conversion, total revenue and net property income increased by 2.9% and 5.0% year on year, respectively, due to appreciation of the Renminbi.

Looking ahead, the policy environment should remain supportive. In mid-2026, the Chinese government elevated consumption to a strategic priority with the five-year plan for expanding consumption, complemented by measures during the year, including expanded personal loan subsidies and incentives for brands opening “first stores”. These measures may help support purchasing power and retailer expansion over time, although it is not yet clear how quickly they will lead to a sustained recovery in consumption. We remain focused on proactive leasing and tenant mix optimisation to keep occupancy and rental income steady.

### **Portfolio Valuation Review**

In respect of the car park located at Wang Fuk Court, Tai Po, the depreciated replacement cost approach has been adopted due to the special circumstances arising from a fire accident.

- The valuation methods are in line with the market practice of property valuation and are in compliance with the Trust Deed and Link’s Compliance Manual.

### **Capital Management**

During the period under review, global financial markets remained volatile as geopolitical tensions and energy-driven inflation reshaped interest rate expectations. In a decisive reversal from the prior year, the US Federal Reserve raised the federal funds target range by 25bps in September 2026, its first hike since 2023, as concerns over energy-driven inflation prompted a more cautious monetary policy stance across several major economies.

### **Corporate Strategy**

— During the period, we continued to execute against the back-to-basics direction focusing — on our core assets shopping malls and car parks in our Asia Pacific markets, disposing of non-core assets, and returning capital to Unitholders while investing to protect the long-term competitiveness of the portfolio.

### **Focusing on the core and divesting non-core assets**

We continued to advance our back-to-basics efforts during the period, with a focus on disciplined capital allocation and enhancing long-term unitholder value. During 1H 2026/2027, we completed the disposals of Swing By @ Thomson Plaza in Singapore and a 50% interest in 100 Market Street in Sydney. We further agreed to dispose of the logistics portfolio in the Chinese Mainland and 700 Nathan Road, Hong Kong subsequent to the financial period end. Non-core asset divestment remains a key priority, and we will continue to optimise our portfolio.

In line with our commitment to returning excess capital to unitholders, we completed approximately HK$1.5 billion of unit buybacks during the period, utilising the proceeds from the disposal of Swing By @ Thomson Plaza. For the second half of 2026/2027, we intend to undertake at least HK$1 billion and up to a maximum of HK$2 billion in unit buybacks, subject to the market valuation of Link units remaining attractive.

Alongside capital returns, we remain focused on reinvesting in our core portfolio to support long-term competitiveness and sustainable growth. With the Hong Kong retail market showing signs of recovery and early signals that spot rents may be stabilising, we see an opportunity to reinvest in our assets through targeted capital expenditure initiatives, enhancing long-term competitiveness and preparedness for a more supportive operating environment.

Consistent with our disciplined capital allocation framework, the deployment of proceeds from future divestments, whether towards reinvestment in core assets, strengthening the balance sheet or returning capital to unitholders, will be assessed and communicated on a transaction-by-transaction basis.

### **Expanding joint ventures and third-party capital partnerships**

We continued to advance our third-party capital management partnerships during the period. Following the partial divestment of 100 Market Street, we have entered into a management service agreement for the investment, generating recurring fee income.

Subsequent to the period end, AUM increased further to approximately HK$13 billion (US$1.65 billion), reflecting the asset management services agreement for Kwai Fong Plaza, the asset management engagement for 700 Nathan Road following its disposal, and the recent partial acquisition of Eastpoint Mall in Singapore, which Link will manage. These developments reflect our ongoing efforts to grow fee-based income streams and deepen our third-party capital partnerships.

While our near-term priority remains the disposal of non-core assets and the deployment of proceeds to create value for Unitholders, we continue to see merit in gradually building Link’s capacity to form and manage joint ventures and raise third-party capital, providing Link with optionality and income growth over the medium to long term.

### **Outlook**

The external environment remains uncertain. Interest rates, market volatility, rising energy costs and geopolitical developments continue to create headwinds. For 2026/2027, we remain focused on maintaining stable earnings. However, the rise in interest rates and utility costs mean that we do not expect full-year earnings to exceed the prior year. Rental reversions in Hong Kong are expected to remain broadly in line with 2025/2026. Looking further ahead to 2027/2028, we expect conditions to remain challenging as our income is rebased at a lower level and due to the enduring macro uncertainty, hawkish monetary policy outlook and inflationary environment. While market rents are starting to stabilise and retail sales show some signs of improvement, it will take some time for this to translate into a recovery in Link REIT’s earnings.

We will therefore maintain strict cost and capital discipline, continue the disposal of non-core assets and reinvest selectively in the existing core portfolio. We will continue to consider buybacks when the unit price is considered relatively attractive and there is surplus capital from divestments. We are confident that these actions will simplify and sharpen our focus, and better position us when market conditions improve.

### **(c) Valuation Techniques**

In valuing the completed properties, the Principal Valuer and the Alternative Valuer have primarily used income capitalisation method ( **Income Capitalisation Method** ) by capitalising the rental income derived from the existing tenancies, if any, with due provision for the potential reversionary income of each constituent portion of the properties at appropriate capitalisation rates. Adjustments have been made to allow for operation expenses, voids and outgoings etc.

The Principal Valuer and the Alternative Valuer have relied on Income Capitalisation Method as the primary method to arrive at the market values of the investment properties and made cross reference to market comparable transactions to ascertain the capital value or the assumed market rent, and in addition, for overseas properties where local valuation standards require, discounted cashflow method.

In respect of the car park located at Wang Fuk Court, Tai Po, within the Hong Kong car parks, the depreciated replacement cost approach has been adopted to arrive at the market value of the property, due to the special circumstances arising from a fire accident in the neighbourhood.

Frequent re-shuffles of the executive directorate, absent the anchor of a controlling Unitholder, may promote “short-termism”. Any risk of entrenchment in office is counter-balanced by the directors of Link, who have the collective power (and the Unitholders also have the same power under the Trust Deed) to remove a recalcitrant executive director of Link.
