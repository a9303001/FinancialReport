Note: **NFA** Net Floor Area, excluding all structural encumbrances and shared building common areas.

**Net Absorption** refers to closing occupied stock minus opening occupied stock. Occupation Permit determines when a building enters completed stock. Pre-leased space contributes to net absorption when recognised as occupied. **New pre-leasing commitments** refer to any leasing signed for upcoming supply before occupation permit is obtained.

The race for premier office space in Hong Kong has reached an inflection point. Driven by a rebound in Chinese IPOs and sustained mainland capital, One and Two IFC are nearing full capacity. Premium Grade-A Central rents surged 27.7% year-onyear in September, with vacancy tumbling from the begining of the year at 14.5% to 6.9% in September. Rental growth in Q3 broadened across the city, with Central, Admiralty, Wan Chai, and Kowloon posting gains between 1.3% and 27.7%.

For the first three quarters, net absorption for Hong Kong Grade-A offices was 139% of 2025 level. With pipeline deliveries remaining modest over the next four years, rents are positioned

on a sustained upward trajectory. This tight supply is forcing tenants to act 12 to 18 months ahead of lease expiries to lock in rates.

This squeeze is fueling decentralisation. With Central availability becoming tight, occupiers requiring expansion space and harbour views will move outward. With vacancy rate low at 4%, waterfront corridors in Admiralty are no longer beneficiary this time around.  We expect gradual spill over to West Kowloon and Causeway Bay due to upcoming completions, and to Quarry Bay due to affordable rents and high vacancy.

#### **HONG KONG ISLAND**

#### **Central: Chinese Financial is the Influx**

Despite expectations of tighter outbound capital rules, mainland financial institutions drove a strong Q3 rebound. Notable decentralisation includes mainland brokerage Guangfa Securities (GF Securities) moved from Wanchai Grade-B office to lease entire high floor of approximately 70,000 sq ft at One IGC (International Gateway Centre) atop the West Kowloon High Speed Rail Station.

#### **KOWLOON**

#### **Kowloon: More relocations ahead**

Relocations accounted for approximately 90% of the top 10 office transactions by floor area, jumping from roughly 55% the previous month. The average transaction size expanded notably in Q3 to 5,400 sq ft, up from 4,000 sq ft in the first half of the year. Rather than renewing with incumbent landlords, occupiers are actively seizing the window to upsize, upgrade, and reposition their footprints to lock in favourable rates ahead of broader rental growth.

On the supply side, the completion of Crystal Tower in Kwun Tong introduced fresh Grade-A space this quarter. With anchor commitments still pending, the newly delivered floorplates add immediate pressure to Kowloon East’s already elevated vacancy rates.

#### **Residential Sales Market**

The market has bottomed since April 2025. Home price rose by 12% from trough and recovered from the multiyear downturn, but the rate-cycle shift has shifted buyer sentiment from recovery to wait-and-see.

Actual mortgage costs have not risen materially yet — this is the key difference versus a full rate-hike transmission during August 2022 to December 2024 where a sharp hike in effective mortgage rate in Hong Kong from rose sharply 1.5% to above 4%.

Watchpoints for the next 3–6 months: whether local banks raise Prime, the path of HIBOR, further Fed action, and primary launch pricing and volume.

Overall, the residential market is expected to remain stable-to-consolidating into year-end rather than re-accelerating. Structural demand and limited supply keep the medium-term bias constructive provided rates do not rise sharply from current effective levels. Price growth is expected to soften and home price to grow by 8-10% year on year.  We continue to believe volume growth to lead price growth. First hand sales to reach 20,000 units by this end

year, and monthly transaction volume of 1,500 units for coming months will comfortably reach out expectation.

Amid US rate hike expectation, developers continue to offer attractive fixed rate mortgage packages to lock in financing cost before potential rate hike. For instance, “first mortgage followed by bank mortgage” schemes during the pre-sale period. These packages often feature fixed interest rates below 3.0%, providing strong incentives for homebuyers and supporting primary market sales.

Despite tighter PRC tax enforcement, there are currently limited signs of a material impact on mainland buyers’ interest in Hong Kong residential property. Demand from mainland purchasers appears to remain broadly resilient, suggesting that other considerations, including investment diversification and housing needs, continue to support buying interest.

Several notable developments stood out during the quarter. The Legacy in Mid-Levels West recorded strong sales momentum, with approximately 20 to 30 units sold within a short period, attracting significant interest from Chinese mainland buyers.

## Retail

### **Inbound Tourism Provides Early Support to Retail and Leasing Activity**

Hong Kong’s retail market gained further momentum, fuelled by stronger sales and visitor demand. Total retail sales grew 8.5% YoY to HK$266 billion in the first eight months, led by a 22.6% YoY increase in jewellery, watches and clocks, and valuable gifts. Visitor arrivals also rose 10.5% YoY to 36.7 million, with Chinese Mainland visitors accounting for around 78% of the total.

The positive momentum continued during the Mainland’s National Day Golden Week from 1 to 7 October. The city welcomed around 1.6 million visitors, including more than 1.4 million from the Chinese Mainland, surpassing last year. Hotel occupancy reached around 90% during the holiday period.

Hotel performance also remained healthy, with average occupancy at 87% from January to August, up one percentage point YoY, while ADR increased 6.9% YoY to HK$1,292. Overall, the sustained growth in visitor arrivals, robust Golden Week traffic and solid hotel performance point to resilient visitordriven consumption, providing support for Hong Kong’s retail market heading into the fourth quarter.

Improving retail and tourism fundamentals continued to drive leasing activity in prime locations. Alo Yoga opened its first flagship store at K11 MUSEA in Tsim Sha Tsui, occupying more than 7,000 sq ft, with tourists accounting for around 60% of spending. In Admiralty, King Fook Holdings committed to a 5,920 sq ft single-brand watch boutique at Pacific Place Two, at an average monthly rent of approximately HK$3.7 million, or HK$630 per sq ft, signalling continued expansion by luxury retailers.
