# Overall office vacancy rates drop to the lowest level since May 2023

Mainly driven by the leasing activities in Central and Tsimshatsui

## Key takeaways

* Overall vacancy rates in Grade A office market declined for the fifth consecutive month, reaching the lowest level since May 2023.
* Central recorded the most significant improvement among the major office submarkets, with its vacancy rate declining from 7.8% to 7.2%.
* Vacancy of top tier office buildings continued to decline as leasing activities are focussed on these buildings in Central, Tsimshatsui and increasingly the new supply in West Kowloon.

**HONG KONG, 26 October 2026** – Overall vacancy rates in Hong Kong’s Grade A office market declined for the fifth consecutive month, reaching the lowest level since May 2023, mainly driven by active leasing activities in Central and Tsimshatsui, according to JLL's latest [Hong Kong Monthly Market Dynamics](/en-hk/insights/market-dynamics/hong-kong-monthly) released today.

Overall vacancy rates declined by 0.1 ppt month-on-month to 12.4% at the end of September. Central recorded the most significant improvement among the major office submarkets, with its vacancy rate declining from 7.8% to 7.2%. Tsimshatsui continued to record improvements in occupancy, while vacancy rates rose modestly across other major submarkets. Wanchai / Causeway Bay registered the largest increase, up 0.6 ppt, followed by Hong Kong East and Kowloon East, where vacancy rates each edged up by 0.2 ppt.

**Sam Gourlay, Head of Office Leasing Advisory at JLL in Hong Kong**, said: "Hong Kong’s Grade A office market recorded a positive net absorption of 245,800 sq ft in September. Vacancy of top tier office buildings continued to decline as leasing activities are focussed on these buildings in Central, Tsimshatsui and increasingly the new supply in West Kowloon”.

The leasing market in September was also highlighted by a major upgrade transaction, with GF Securities leasing a linked floor totalling 78,872 sq ft at International Gateway Centre in West Kowloon and relocating from GF Tower in Wanchai. The move reflects the growing appeal of West Kowloon among securities firms, supported by its proximity to the High Speed Rail Station, which provides more convenient access to high-net-worth clients from the Mainland China.

**Cathie Chung, Senior Director of Research at JLL**, added: "Grade A office rents rose 0.7% m-o-m, primarily driven by a 1.6% increase in Central. Rents across other major submarkets were largely stable, except in Kowloon East, where rents fell 0.6%."
