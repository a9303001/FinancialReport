# Summary

The HKSAR Government has released a proposal to waive the stamp duty on transfer of (i) Hong Kong non-residential properties and (ii) shares in non-residential property-holding special purpose vehicles (SPVs) into real estate investment trusts (REITs) seeking to list in the Hong Kong SAR (Hong Kong).

In this tax alert, we discuss the key features of the proposed stamp duty waiver and share our observations.

---

# Background

To promote the REIT market and encourage more REITs to list in Hong Kong, the 2026/27 Budget1 announced that a stamp duty waiver will be introduced to facilitate the transfer of non-residential properties into REITs seeking to list in Hong Kong. The HKSAR Government has recently issued a Legislative Council paper2 setting out the framework for the proposed stamp duty waiver.

# The key features of the proposed stamp duty waiver

***1. Eligible REITs***

* The stamp duty waiver would only apply to (i) **new REITs seeking to list on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange** and (ii) REITs already listed on a non-Hong Kong exchange that are seeking a **dual-primary or secondary listing in Hong Kong** (i.e. “eligible REITs”). Existing REITs already listed in Hong Kong will not be eligible.
* For the waiver to apply, the eligible REITs must be listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange **within six months** after the assignment of the Relevant Properties (as defined below) or the transfer of the Relevant Shares (as defined below). Flexibility on this time limit may be allowed in exceptional cases.

***2. Stamp duty to be waived***

* The waiver applies to stamp duty imposed on the transfer of (i) non-residential properties located in Hong Kong3 (Relevant Properties) and (ii) shares in SPVs that are Hong Kong stocks4 for stamp duty purposes, provided that non-residential properties account for **at least 95%** of the value of the assets held by the SPV5 (Relevant Shares).
* The stamp duty waiver will not apply to the Relevant Properties or Relevant Shares transferred into a REIT after its date of listing in Hong Kong.

***3. Minimum property-holding period***

The Relevant Properties or Relevant Shares must be the scheme property of the eligible REIT from the date of its first listing and remain as such until **two years** after the date of the relevant instrument of transfer.

***4. Minimum listing period***

The eligible REITs must remain listed in Hong Kong for at least **two years**.

***5. Safeguard measures***

* Where the stamp duty is waived, the following safeguards would be put in place:
  + For transfer of Relevant Properties, a statutory charge would be created on the relevant properties, and
  + For transfer of Relevant Shares, the eligible REIT would be required to provide security to the Government.
* The eligible REIT can apply for the release of the charge/security after fulfilling all the conditions for the stamp duty waiver.

# Legislative timeline and the transitional arrangement

The Government plans to introduce the relevant Amendment Bill into the Legislative Council in the **first half of 2027**.

To encourage early listing, the Government proposes that stamp duty paid on transfer of Relevant Properties / Relevant Shares into an eligible REIT from the date on which the relevant Amendment Bill is introduced into the Legislative Council to the date on which the corresponding Amendment Ordinance comes into effect (i.e. the transitional period) will be refunded, provided that the REIT fulfils the eligibility criteria.

# KPMG observations

* The Government’s proposal is a welcomed move for enhancing Hong Kong’s competitiveness as a listing venue for REITs and promoting the growth of the REIT market in Hong Kong.
* According to the Legislative Council paper, the transitional period will commence on the date the relevant Amendment Bill is introduced into the Legislative Council. Further elaboration from the Government on the rationale for using that date, rather than the date on which the Amendment Bill is published in the Gazette, as the start date would be welcomed.
* Interested sponsors, asset owners and prospective REIT issuers should closely monitor the legislative process and pay attention to any further guidance on the operation of the waiver in practice. In particular, clarifications from the Government on the following issues would be helpful:

   i. what concrete evidence is required to demonstrate that the REIT concerned is actively progressing towards listing in Hong Kong;
   ii. the factors that will be considered in deciding whether to grant an extension beyond the six‑month listing deadline; and
   iii. how the 95% minimum non-residential property holding threshold for SPVs will be applied (e.g. the timing and basis for determining the value of the non-residential properties and other assets held by the SPV).
