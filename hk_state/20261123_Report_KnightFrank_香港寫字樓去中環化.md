## 1. Executive Summary

The V-shaped rebound in Premium Central has slammed the door on cheap core space. After a grinding 36-month downcycle, Premium Central rents surged 28.8% from their August 2025 trough to HK$130 psf in September 2026— erasing three years of declines in just 13 months. Core vacancy has dropped from 14.5% at the start of the year to 6.9%, driven by a resurgence in mainland IPOs alongside growth in the asset management and private wealth sectors. With flagship

assets like One and Two IFC effectively at full occupancy and effective rents climbing, the flight-to-quality phase is rapidly hitting a capacity ceiling.

Upcoming tight supply makes decentralisation inevitable once again. Unlike in previous cycles, core-adjacent options are scarce: Admiralty’s waterfront corridor sits at a tight 4% vacancy and cannot absorb large expansion mandates. As a result, two distinct trends are emerging.

#### **Two distinct submarkets are poised to capture this outward spillover:**

**De-Centralisation:** Occupiers requiring large floorplates, premier specifications, and harbour views are increasingly looking outward. The rental gap has reopened aggressively, with the Central-tofringe discount widening back to multi-year highs and shifting corporate strategies from core consolidation back to cost-effective decentralisation.

- **West Kowloon (The Scale & Institutional Magnet):** Anchored by ICC, IGC, and the Artist Square Towers, West Kowloon forms a consolidated institutional enclave of 4.2 million sq ft net effective area—equivalent to 100% of Admiralty’s total office footprint, 80% of Premium Central, and 70% of Core TST. With its rental discount against Central expanding to HK$35 psf, it stands as the primary destination for investment banks, tech majors, and PRC asset managers requiring contiguous expansion floors and High-Speed Rail connectivity.

- **Causeway Bay (The Financial & Professional Services Hub):** Underpinned by the completion of Caroline Hill Road (Lee Garden Eight), the district is again in the spotlight. Anchored by pre-leasing commitments from ZA Bank and multiple follow-on leases secured by local law firms in November, occupancy has escalated sharply—capturing financial and professional services tenants seeking new and large floor space within Hong Kong Island.

**Re-centralisation:** Conversely, occupiers with strong stickiness to Central can choose to stay or relocate back to lock in rents before they climb further. Tenants can still secure prime core space by tapping into existing vacancies and confirmed upcoming exits, including Jane Street’s departure from Chater House (105,000 sq ft NFA), as well as available space in Cheung Kong Center I (approx. 25% vacancy) and Exchange Square I & II (approx. 10% vacancy, or roughly 100,000 sq ft).

## 2. The Cost of the Core: Rising Rents and Aging Infrastructure

### **Grade-A office supply in Central is tightening**

The race for premier office space in Hong Kong has reached an inflection point. Driven by a rebound in Chinese IPOs and sustained mainland capital inflows, One and Two IFC are nearing full capacity. While it took roughly two years to fill recent additions such as Cheung Kong Center II and The Henderson, expansion from asset management and securities firms has emerged as a major driver of the current leasing rally.

The primary catalyst behind this wave is the rapid growth of quantitative trading firms, multi-strategy hedge funds, and private capital managers. Fuelled by regional trading activity and the consolidation of APAC headquarters in Hong Kong, firms including SIG, Point72, Qube Research & Technologies, and Jane Street have aggressively leased large, contiguous floorplates across Central. SFC Type 9 License, continuous growth in asset management firm set up since 2023 has a track record as a leading indicator of such trend.

This momentum is no longer confined to Central; it is generating a clear spillover effect across the wider market. Over the first three quarters of 2026, net absorption for Hong Kong Grade-A offices reached 139% of 2025’s fullyear level, already outpacing total new supply for 2026. With pipeline deliveries remaining modest over the next four years—and new Central supply only arriving in 2027 and 2031 onwards—rents are positioned on a sustained upward trajectory.

At the same time, as core rents climb and contiguous prime space grows scarce, occupiers seeking larger,

modern floorplates are increasingly looking beyond traditional Central—where the average building age is around 40 years—toward emerging commercial hubs such as Causeway Bay and West Kowloon. Meanwhile, several law firms have relocated to Quarry Bay to capture meaningful cost savings.

The rent gap between Central and decentralised districts has widened due to surging core rents. Relocating to West Kowloon, Causeway Bay, or Quarry Bay can save occupiers up to HK$35–66 per sq ft, prompting some existing Central tenants to decentralise.

## 3. The Great Relocation: Who Is Moving Where?

### **CBD Outward Relocations (2024 – 2026 Q3)**

The CBD relocation trend is no longer a broad, fragmented scatter—it is a concentrated, one-way flight across Victoria Harbour. Between 2024 and Q3 2026, over NFA of 896,126 sq ft office space relocated out of the core CBD (Central, Admiralty, Sheung Wan). The relocation map reveals sharp, district-by-district polarities:

**CBD Occupiers Relocating** to Other Districts by Committed NFA of New Leases (2024–2026 Q3)

### **1. Central: The West Kowloon Runaway (90% Captured)**

When Central occupiers relocate, they don’t downscale to fringe Hong Kong Island—they cross the harbour in bulk.

- Destination of Choice: West Kowloon captured a crushing 90% of all outbound Central NFA (530,857 sq ft).

- The Remainder: Minor spillovers trickled into Sheung Wan (5%, 31,660 sq ft), Wong Chuk Hang (2%), and Admiralty (2%).

Central tenants are executing large-footprint consolidations. Rather than compromising on building grade or fragmenting operations, core occupiers are trading Central postcodes directly for institutional scale and High-Speed Rail connectivity in West Kowloon.

### **2. Sheung Wan: The High-Volume Cross-Harbour Split (43% to West Kowloon)**

#### **Sheung Wan saw substantial displacement across 357,099 sq ft net of outbound moves, acting as a feeder pool across the city:**

- Top : West Kowloon took the lion’s share at 43% (154,411 sq ft).

- Island East & Fringe Dispersal: Wan Chai absorbed 11% (38,183 sq ft), Wong Chuk Hang took 10% (37,344 sq ft), Causeway Bay captured 8%, and Quarry Bay took 6%.

- Core Upgrades: Notably, 18% (64,100 sq ft across 9 transactions) relocated back into Central, capturing boutique upgrades during the market trough.

Mid-market financial and professional firms in Sheung Wan either seized the window to upgrade into Central or decamped entirely to West Kowloon and South Island for cost relief.

### **3. Admiralty: Local Clustered Upgrades (Wan Chai & Central Dominate)**

#### **Admiralty occupiers stayed firmly tied to the core corridor, showing near-zero appetite for decentralized fringe markets:**

- The Split: Relocations were overwhelmingly anchored next door: Central absorbed 38% (37,656 sq ft) and Wan Chai captured 32% (31,184 sq ft).

- Cross-Harbour: West Kowloon secured 16% (15,591 sq ft).

- Island East: A negligible 5% moved to North Point and 3% to Causeway Bay.

Admiralty occupiers demonstrate strong locational inertia. They are either upgrading into core Central or shifting one MTR stop east to Wan Chai to preserve proximity to government and institutional hubs.

## 6. De-centralisation or Re-centralisation ? To Relocate or Not to Relocate

A sharp rebound in Premium Central—only took 13 months to catch up the loss in previous cycle of 36 months—has abruptly closed the tenant-favourable window, forcing occupiers into a strategic dilemma: renewal or relocate.

Staying in Central requires absorbing immediate rental spikes to lock in leases before escalating scarcity and further rate increases take hold. Conversely, decentralising unlocks a compelling arbitrage of HK$35–66 psf by relocating to West Kowloon, Causeway Bay, or Quarry Bay.

With core concessions vanishing, corporate decisionmakers must weigh paying rising premiums for Central prestige against capturing scale and massive cost savings across Victoria Harbour. The next decentralisation and recentralisation wave has arrived.
