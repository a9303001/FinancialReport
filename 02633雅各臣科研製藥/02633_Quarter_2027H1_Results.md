### **MANAGEMENT DISCUSSION AND ANALYSIS** 

### **BUSINESS REVIEW** 

The Group’s business in essential medicines and specialty drugs returned to growth in the Reporting Period. Sales rose by 6.2% following a broadly stable performance compared with FY2026 Interim. The Private Sector accounted for approximately 70.5% of the increase, with positive growth on medicines for cardiovascular conditions, gastrointestinal disorders and cytotoxic chemotherapy. Public Sector sales also grew, supported by cardiovascular medicines, neuropathic pain drugs and a recovery in demand for cold and cough preparations. 

Hong Kong’s summer influenza season began in late June, compared with early September a year earlier. The Centre for Health Protection reported that influenza activity rose from July to mid-August. The earlier onset supported stronger demand for the Group’s cold and cough medicines during the Reporting Period. 

Demand for chronic disease medicines also strengthened. Cardiovascular therapies sustained the growth seen in FY2026, with higher sales of beta-blockers to both sectors and of anticoagulants to the Public Sector. The wider use of community screening and follow-up care is particularly relevant to the Group’s portfolio of medicines for chronic conditions. 

The Group broadened its portfolio through launches in oncology supportive care and central nervous system therapies, while 20 new products secured registration approval. Higher output from existing manufacturing facilities, particularly of sterile eye drops, supported current supply as the Group advanced its plans for additional manufacturing capacity at Tai Po InnoPark. 

Guided by its “5 to Thrive” sustainability framework, the Group continued to pursue its established targets for reducing greenhouse gas emissions, waste generation, energy consumption and water use across its operations. During the Reporting Period, the Group supported pharmacy education and research through a scholarship and awards at a local university’s medical faculty. The Group also supported a charitable initiative for young people with disabilities, while community organisations received donations. 

### **RESULTS** 

During the Reporting Period, the Group’s total sales revenue across the Public and Private Sectors grew by 6.2% to HK$813.9 million from HK$766.6 million in the corresponding period last year, with both the Public and Private Sectors contributing to the increase. 

Gross profit rose to HK$339.9 million from HK$337.8 million in the corresponding period last year. Profit attributable to equity shareholders of the Company increased by 2.1% to HK$145.1 million from HK$142.0 million, while adjusted EBITDA rose to HK$280.7 million from HK$260.2 million. 

As at 30 September 2026, the Group’s cash and cash equivalents stood at HK$441.1 million, up from HK$369.9 million at 31 March 2026. Net gearing declined to 14.1% from 20.8% over the same period.

### **OPERATING PERFORMANCE** 

### **Performance across Core Therapeutic Areas** 

Hong Kong’s ageing population and the growing emphasis on early detection and management of chronic diseases such as hypertension and diabetes continued to support demand for longterm treatment. During the Reporting Period, the Group recorded sales growth across several therapeutic areas, with contributions from both the Public and Private Sectors. 

Cardiovascular medicines continued to contribute to growth in both the Public and Private Sectors, reflecting sustained demand for chronic disease medicines following FY2026. Sales of beta-blockers increased in both sectors. Public Sector sales of anticoagulants also rose, while Private Sector growth was evident in combination antihypertensives, including amlodipine/valsartan combination tablets. 

In the Public Sector, sales also grew across oncology, gastrointestinal and central nervous system therapies. The Group recorded higher sales of hormonal treatments for cancer, including bicalutamide, exemestane and letrozole, as well as antispasmodics and hypnotics. Sales of medicines for neuropathic pain increased strongly, with the Group also securing a Public Sector tender for pregabalin capsules during the Reporting Period. Alongside these gains, sales of cold and cough medicines rose during the summer influenza season. 

In the Private Sector, sales of cardiovascular and gastrointestinal medicines grew strongly. The Group also increased sales of oral arsenic trioxide within its cytotoxic chemotherapy portfolio and expanded its antiemetic offering through the introduction of aprepitant capsules during the Reporting Period. Growth extended to several established categories used in private practice, including laxatives, asthma medicines, and emollients and skin protectants. 

### **Product Launches and R&D Pipeline** 

New product launches during the Reporting Period included aprepitant capsules, which broadened the Group’s oncology supportive care range, and aripiprazole tablets, pramipexole tablets, pregabalin capsules and gabapentin capsules, which extended its central nervous system portfolio. Separately, the Group obtained registration approval for 20 new products, paving the way for their commercial launch. 

The Group’s R&D pipeline expanded from 241 products as at 31 March 2026 to 248 as at 30 September 2026. The seven additions during the Reporting Period broadened the portfolio under development in areas including cardiovascular disease, central nervous system disorders, allergy and pain management. 

The Group also advanced products already in development. Five products entered stability testing during the Reporting Period. By period end, 11 products had completed development and been submitted to the Department of Health of Hong Kong for registration. These submissions included treatments for migraine, gastrointestinal disorders and diabetes. 

– 15 – 

The number of products awaiting registration rose from 10 as at 31 March 2026 to 21 as at 30 September 2026. A further 71 products were in preparation for stability testing or undergoing testing at period end, compared with 67 at the previous financial year end. 

### **Manufacturing Operations and Capacity** 

Equipment upgrades, automation and greater use of existing production lines increased output at the Group’s established manufacturing facilities. These improvements supported the supply of medicines for chronic conditions, paediatric formulations and seasonal cold and cough preparations to hospitals and private practitioners. 

Specialised ophthalmic preparations remained a key area of growth in the Group’s high-value sterile manufacturing operations. During the Reporting Period, sterile eye drop output rose by approximately 21.2% year on year to 28,910 litres, following an increase of approximately 7.8% in the full year ended 31 March 2026. 

Production also increased across solid and liquid dosage forms. Output of tablets and capsules grew by approximately 11.6% year on year to 1.7 billion units, while liquid medicine production rose by approximately 9.7% to 1.0 million litres. Semi-solid output totalled 105.2 tonnes. Greater use of production lines at one subsidiary gave the Group more flexibility in scheduling the manufacture of dermatological and other topical preparations. 

### **Smart Manufacturing at Tai Po InnoPark** 

Building on equipment upgrades and automation plans that have strengthened its manufacturing capacity and capabilities, the Group is advancing its transition to smart manufacturing through the development of its Tai Po InnoPark facility. The Group secured the purpose-built pharmaceutical facility through a successful tender in February 2025 and appointed the main contractor for the design-and-build works during FY2026. 

Supported by the Hong Kong Government’s New Industrialisation Acceleration Scheme, the facility spans over 100,000 square feet and is designed to accommodate ten smart production lines for sterile eye drops, solid dosage forms and oral liquid medicines. By combining robotics and automation with real-time production data, the Group aims to strengthen process control and maintain consistent product quality as production expands. The planned oral liquid line will also support the Group’s collaboration with a medical school on specialty leukaemia treatments. 

The facility’s design incorporates energy-efficient systems, environmental monitoring and measures to manage water and waste, in line with the Group’s “5 to Thrive” sustainability framework.

### **BUSINESS DEVELOPMENT** 

### **In-licensing of Specialty Drugs** 

The Group entered into in-licensing agreements covering 20 specialty drugs during the Reporting Period. Building on its in-licensing progress in FY2026, the Group continued to select therapies that complement its own development programme and address clinical needs in hospitals and specialist practice. 

Oncology and supportive care remained a focus, with agreements covering targeted oral therapies for solid tumours, long-acting hormonal treatments for advanced malignancies and biologic agents for the management of cancer-related skeletal complications. The Group also secured rights to oral therapies for fibrotic lung conditions. 

In cardiovascular care, combination therapies for chronic heart failure and fixed-dose lipidlowering combinations complemented the Group’s existing range of antihypertensives and other cardiovascular medicines. In immunology and critical care, the Group expanded its range of plasma-derived therapies for specialist and hospital use by securing rights to human normal immunoglobulin and serum albumin infusions in multiple concentrations. 

The agreements also extended to gastroenterology, hepatology and neurology. Injectable treatments for acute gastrointestinal bleeding and hepatorenal complications broadened the Group’s gastroenterology and hepatology offerings, while extended-release treatments for attention-deficit disorders complemented its neurology portfolio. 

### **Advancing the Yikaida CAR-T Programme** 

The Group’s collaboration with Shanghai Fosun Pharmaceutical (Group) Co., Ltd.* (上海復 星醫藥（集團）股份有限公司) and Shanghai Fosun Kairos Biotechnology Co., Ltd.* (復星 凱瑞（上海）生物科技有限公司) established a treatment and logistics pathway for Yikaida (Axicabtagene Ciloleucel) in Hong Kong and Macau. The CAR-T therapy is used in the treatment of relapsed or refractory diffuse large B-cell lymphoma. Its delivery requires coordination between the treating centre and the specialist logistics team, from collection of the patient’s white blood cells by apheresis to the return of the prepared cells for reinfusion. The Group’s licensed advanced therapy product logistics platform supports this process. 

Following the first patient reinfusion in Hong Kong in March 2025, the programme extended to Macau in September 2025. As at 30 September 2026, six patients in Hong Kong and four in Macau had completed both apheresis and reinfusion on a cumulative basis, compared with three in Hong Kong and one in Macau at 31 March 2026. 

### **Digital Procurement through e-Jacob Pharma2U** 

Clinic registrations on e-Jacob Pharma2U continued to grow during the Reporting Period, building on a base of over 1,200 registered clinics at the end of the previous financial year. The Group’s proprietary mobile and web ordering platform provides a direct channel for serving general practitioners, specialists, dentists and veterinarians, whose purchasing needs vary by type of practice.

### **OUTLOOK** 

Hong Kong’s ageing population and wider access to screening are expected to sustain demand for affordable medicines for long-term conditions. As more patients receive follow-up care in the community, the Group sees opportunities to build on the growth of its cardiovascular portfolio and broaden the supply of medicines for metabolic conditions through hospitals and clinics. 

Hong Kong’s first Five-Year Plan envisages an increase of about 30% in resources allocated to primary healthcare programmes by 2030/31. District Health Centres and the Primary Healthcare Co-care Network connect patients identified through screening with family doctors for ongoing care. Provisional figures published after the Reporting Period illustrate the scale of this development. By 7 October 2026, more than 300,000 residents had enrolled in the network, and approximately 70,000 had entered the treatment phase. 

The Community Pharmacy Programme could extend the Group’s reach by enabling patients to obtain prescribed medicines and refills closer to home. On 23 October 2026, the Health Bureau of Hong Kong announced that patient pairing would begin by the end of that month, initially through 20 designated pharmacies across four districts. As the programme expands, the Group will review its registered portfolio against the Community Drug Formulary, identify medicines suited to community supply and assess tender opportunities as procurement requirements become clear. 

The 2026 Policy Address also set out plans to establish the Centre for Medical Products Regulation and to support multicentre studies through the Greater Bay Area International Clinical Trial Institute. These developments offer the Group and its licensing partners scope to explore clinical collaborations and generate evidence to support the introduction of specialty medicines in Hong Kong. 

In the second half, the Group will focus on preparing newly approved products for launch, advancing registration submissions and pursuing selected specialty in-licensing opportunities. Its existing manufacturing facilities will continue to support the supply of chronic disease medicines and respond to seasonal demand for cold and cough preparations. In parallel, the Group will advance its smart manufacturing project at Tai Po InnoPark to expand longer-term capacity for essential and specialty medicines.
